# CV Reviewer Verdict — UpSlide, Senior Product Manager (AI agents/workflows, financial deliverables)

## 1. Would this CV get a callback?
**Borderline / leaning No.** The domain fit (a decade+ inside finance, deep familiarity with how banks and asset managers actually build PowerPoint/Excel/Word deliverables) is genuinely strong and rare, but the candidate has zero demonstrated experience shipping an AI product that *specifically* automates or refines Office documents — the one broker-voting "AI-native" project is a web app, not a PowerPoint/Excel/Word agent, and the CV itself flags "no direct prior experience building an AI-agent evaluation/QA loop... for agent output" as a residual gap. A skeptical screener hiring for a document-automation-agent PM will read this as "great finance-domain PM, unproven on the specific product surface (Office file generation/refinement via AI agents)" and likely pass it to a second-tier pile rather than fast-track it.

## 2. Exaggerated / vague / unverifiable claims
- **"shipping a broker-voting web application end-to-end on a full AI Software Development Lifecycle, from design to production in under two months"** — "full AI Software Development Lifecycle" is buzzwordy and unverifiable; no metrics on scope, team size, or what "AI" contributed to the SDLC itself (is this about AI-assisted coding, or an AI product feature?). Ambiguous enough that a recruiter can't tell if this is a one-person prototype or a real production system with users.
- **"direct, hands-on fluency in how finance teams build and rely on PowerPoint decks, Excel models, and client-facing deliverables"** — this is inferred/asserted from adjacent roles (sales engineering, account management, presales) rather than a role where the candidate personally built the automation tooling for these deliverables. It conflates "worked around people who make decks" with "product experience automating deck/model creation."
- **"increasing sales-qualified opportunities by 65%"** — no baseline, timeframe, or attribution given; classic unverifiable percentage-lift claim from a presales role, plausible but not checkable.
- **"Delivered AI proof-of-concepts (agentic matching/automation) addressing consulting-staffing challenges"** — vague on outcome/adoption; POCs that didn't ship don't demonstrate product-market delivery capability.
- **"vibe-coded" prototyping** — informal phrasing that undercuts rigor; combined with "open-sourced full-stack application prototypes," there's no indication of usage, stars, or real users, so it reads as personal-project filler rather than product evidence.
- Freelance title "**Product & AI Strategy Lead**" self-assigned since Jul 2025 — self-titled freelance roles are inherently harder to verify than a role at a named employer with a manager reference.

## 3. One concrete change that would most raise callback odds
Add a headline bullet (ideally the first bullet under the most recent role, or a new bullet in the summary) that names a **specific AI-agent-driven automation of an Office deliverable** — e.g., "Built/prototyped an agent that auto-generates/refines [X] PowerPoint slide or Excel model from [data source], reducing manual prep time by [Y]%" — even if it's a personal project or a scoped POC, as long as it's real and traceable. Right now the CV's strongest asset (finance-deck/model domain knowledge) and its AI-agent skillset are described in parallel but never fused into one concrete artifact; UpSlide is hiring for exactly that fusion, and without a single line proving the candidate has actually pointed an agent at a PPTX/XLSX/DOCX file, the CV reads as "adjacent" rather than "direct hit."
