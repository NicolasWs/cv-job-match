## Project Storytelling (STAR) — 3 stories, ~45-60s each

### 1. Opensee compliance delivery (ISO 27001/SOC 2) — headline metric: 99% uptime vs. 95% contracted
**Situation:** Opensee's first SaaS deployment needed to meet HSBC's specific security/compliance bar (ISO 27001, partial SOC 2) before go-live — a hard regulatory gate, not a nice-to-have.
**Task:** Own the compliance delivery workstream and keep the CTO informed weekly on risk and status.
**Action:** Worked directly with DevOps to map requirements to concrete technical controls, prioritized the compliance backlog alongside the product backlog, and structured weekly CTO reporting so blockers surfaced early rather than at the gate.
**Result:** Technical go-live achieved on schedule; contractual uptime commitment (95%) exceeded at over 99% across the first six months.
**Learning:** Regulatory requirements are easiest to hit when they're treated as a first-class workstream with its own tracker from day one, not bolted on before launch.

### 2. Sophis steering committee delivery — headline metric: 7 projects, 25+ consultants coordinated
**Situation:** Sophis needed to deliver a portfolio of implementations and upgrades (portfolio management, risk, order management, compliance systems) across multiple European clients concurrently.
**Task:** Structure and drive delivery of 7 projects, chairing steering committees and coordinating a 25+ person multi-disciplinary team (IT, toolkit developers, Front/Middle/Back Office).
**Action:** Built and maintained project plans and decision logs per engagement, ran steering committee reviews to surface and resolve cross-team blockers, and coordinated resourcing across concurrent projects.
**Result:** All 7 projects delivered (2 full implementations, 5 upgrades) across Europe.
**Learning:** Governance cadence (a standing steering committee with a real decision log) is what actually prevents multi-team projects from drifting — not just having a plan, but having a forcing function to revisit it.

### 3. Opensee early-stage product structuring — headline metric: 100+ users onboarded, HSBC as first cloud/AM client
**Situation:** Opensee needed to define product-market fit and structure the rollout for its first cloud, first-asset-manager client (HSBC GAM) — a genuinely undefined target solution at the outset.
**Task:** Define use cases, key metrics, and the onboarding path from scratch, then coordinate the workstreams (technical, training, compliance) to deliver it.
**Action:** Identified high-value use cases directly with HSBC stakeholders, defined success metrics (reporting automation, data transformation adoption), then designed and delivered technical workshops reaching 100+ business and IT users.
**Result:** Successful onboarding of Opensee's flagship reference client, smooth handover to customer success.
**Learning:** In an undefined-solution environment, the fastest way to get traction is structuring the use cases with the actual users before locking the technical plan.

**Talking points (4-5, tuned to this role):**

- Regulatory/compliance delivery as a first-class workstream, not an afterthought (ISO27001/SOC2 experience maps directly to the posting's compliance line).
- Comfortable structuring an undefined target solution — Opensee's early-stage rollout is the direct precedent for "product design support" on a not-yet-fully-specified initiative.
- Cross-geography, cross-seniority stakeholder management is a repeated pattern (NYC/Brussels/Amsterdam/Paris/Verona; CTO-level reporting; steering committee chairs).
- Honest positioning on primary-markets: strong adjacent capital-markets depth (post-trade/risk/portfolio management), real gap on primary-issuance specifics — frame as fast ramp-up, not blind spot.

1. What does success look like for this initiative at the 6-month mark, and who ultimately owns that call — the Project Lead, or a steering committee?
2. How much of the target product/solution is already decided vs. still open for this role to help shape?
3. What's the current state of cross-team alignment — is Legal/Regulatory/Technology already bought in, or is part of this role building that alignment from scratch?
4. What does the escalation path look like when a workstream is blocked on a decision outside this role's authority?
5. Is there a plan for how this Project Manager role evolves as the initiative matures — toward Product Owner, or staying PM-focused?
